\section*{Motivation}
Middle-mile shipments run on fixed schedules, but a destination hub often learns how late an inbound trip is only on arrival, which limits dock-door and labor planning. Weather is a major driver: the FAA attributes 74.26\% of system-impacting delays over 15 minutes to weather \cite{faa}. We build a streaming pipeline that predicts arrival delay \emph{at departure time} from weather and lane history, and measure how fast and how well it does so.

\section*{Objectives and Goals}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (accuracy).} Do departure delay, streaming weather and lane history beat the ``delay unchanged'' baseline and the historical lane mean (MAE, RMSE)? Models: Spark MLlib Gradient-Boosted Trees and Random Forest, trained on 2018--2023 (2020 excluded), tested on 2024, with features known at departure only. Predicted ETA $=$ scheduled arrival $+$ departure delay $+$ predicted change.
\item \textbf{RQ2 (system performance).} How do cores (2/4/8), shuffle partitions (8/64/200), Kafka replay rate (1k/5k/20k events/s) and \texttt{maxOffsetsPerTrigger} (5k/20k/100k) change throughput, batch time and p50/p95 end-to-end latency \cite{karimov,fedorovych}?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (stretch).} Can an LLM agent answer dispatcher questions from the live prediction tables, kept off the streaming path?
\end{itemize}

\section*{Data Sources}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{BTS Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance, 2018--2024}: tens of millions of flights with scheduled and actual times, delays and delay causes; raw CSV expected in the 5--10\,GB range (to be confirmed after download).\\
\url{https://www.transtats.bts.gov/DL_SelectFields.aspx?gnoyr_VQ=FGJ&QO_fu146_anzr=b0-gvzr}
\item \textbf{NOAA Integrated Surface Database} \cite{isd}: hourly temperature, visibility and wind; free bulk access.\\
\url{https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/global-hourly/}
\item \textbf{Fusion:} each airport is mapped to its nearest NOAA station (ICAO match), local times are converted to UTC, and Spark joins on (station, UTC hour). BTS's weather-delay field is excluded, since it is known only after arrival and covers extreme weather only.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Proxy and limits.} Real middle-mile telematics are proprietary, so scheduled hub-to-hub flights are a public proxy with the same structure (fixed lane, schedule, weather, destination congestion). BTS covers passenger carriers, not cargo carriers. Trucks add Hours of Service limits, road traffic and dwell; the stream replays recorded data. Related work: flight delays \cite{sternberg,beltman}, logistics ETA \cite{wani}; we found no study validating flights as a trucking proxy.

\section*{Architecture, Tools and Plan}
\textbf{Ingestion:} Kafka producer replaying flights in WheelsOff order \cite{kreps}. \textbf{Storage:} Parquet medallion (bronze raw events, silver cleaned and weather-enriched, gold features, predictions and sliding-window hub arrival counts). \textbf{Processing:} PySpark Structured Streaming \cite{armbrust} with stream-static joins and MLlib scoring. \textbf{Serving:} FastAPI and Streamlit dashboard, packaged with Docker Compose (one-command run). Optional stateful feature: previous-leg delay for the same tail number.\\
\textbf{Schedule (8 weeks):} W1 setup; W1--2 data and join; W2--4 features and models; W2--5 producer and streaming job; W4--6 API and dashboard; W5--7 benchmarks; W7--8 report and demo. If compute is tight, Random Forest tuning is cut first.
